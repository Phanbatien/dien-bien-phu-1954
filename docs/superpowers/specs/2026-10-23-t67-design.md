# T67 — lightweight combat effects and scene fades

## Selected approach

The user selected lightweight effects made within this Unity project. An imported effects pack was considered, but would introduce extra assets, licensing and rendering dependencies. Project-made effects provide a small, consistent baseline that can be tuned for the existing night/fog environments.

## What the player will see

- A brief warm muzzle flash when a player or enemy weapon fires, including cannon fire. Player flashes stay near the weapon and do not cover the scope view.
- Small dust puffs on ordinary bullet hits and short sparks on metal hits.
- A brief bright burst followed by a small smoke cloud when grenades, bunkers, tank shells, tanks or the A1 event explode.
- Existing mission fog remains in use; no large transparent smoke layers are added across the map.
- A short fade through black when deploying, returning to the menu, restarting or advancing to another mission. It works while the game is paused and prevents repeated transition requests.

## Technical choices proposed by the implementer

Use built-in particle systems with small procedurally created textures/materials compatible with URP. Reuse a fixed number of effect instances per scene; recycle them under heavy fire. Effects use independent visual randomness and do not change damage, ammunition, mission rules or audio. Existing authored effect overrides remain usable. Smoke and flash lifetimes are bounded; scene changes clean up the old effects.

Transitions use an overlay that survives the scene load and fades using unscaled time. Keep the fade short, preserve tutorial/pause behavior, and release any transition-owned input lock on success or failure.

## Finishing criteria

1. Real firing and explosion paths produce the intended effects in Sandbox/M1 and the relevant later mission paths, including A1.
2. Particle counts and live objects stay bounded under sustained fire; effects pause with world freeze and do not leak across scenes.
3. Transitions fade through black, reach the requested scene and restore input; repeated clicks do not start multiple loads.
4. Focused behavior tests and the full existing Unity suites pass.
5. Inspect screenshots or live frames of muzzle flashes, impacts, explosion/smoke and transitions; fix obvious clipping, opaque squares or missing materials.
6. Measure matched firing runs in the same scene, camera position and settings with effects off/on after warmup. Record frame timings/FPS, settings and hardware context. Required difference: no more than 5 FPS. If that cannot be demonstrated reliably, report the measurement limitation and do not claim full T67 acceptance.
7. Record evidence and update the task tracker only to the level demonstrated by verification.
